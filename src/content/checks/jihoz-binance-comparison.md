## Where the comparison fails

Binance funding a wallet would be Binance's choice, with Binance's money. These payments were a pool's decision. They went to the address that joined the pool, and they began well before the hack.

## The part that does not go away

These payments were **not automatic**, and readers should know that.

- **Each payout was a separate transaction.** It listed recipients and amounts, and two owners signed it.
- **The timing was irregular.** Payouts landed anywhere from half a day to about 47 days apart.
- **The list was edited often.** Across 71 regular payouts it changed 49 times, 30 of them after the hack.
- **This address stayed on it.** The wallet received 39 payouts after the hack, totalling 52,632 RON.

So the signers could have replaced this address at any payout, and Jihoz could have asked them to. It is a fair question why that did not happen, and the chain does not answer it. They may not have been asked. They may not have noticed. Or whoever holds the key may have seen no reason to change it, and that could include Jihoz.

Jihoz's reply suggests these payments were not being followed closely. On November 2, Jihoz wrote that one transaction was "a scatter, could be a refund for something." That is Jihoz's statement, and we cannot verify what Jihoz knew.

A question about why an address was never updated is not evidence that a theft was faked.
